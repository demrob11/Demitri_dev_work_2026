## 🧮 Two's Complement Explained in Detail

Two's complement is the **standard way to represent signed integers** in binary used by virtually all modern computers. It enables computers to perform addition, subtraction, and bitwise operations consistently on both positive and negative numbers.

---

## 🔍 Why Do We Need Two's Complement?

Computers operate using binary (0s and 1s). While unsigned integers are easy (they're just binary), signed integers require a scheme to represent both positive and negative numbers **in the same fixed number of bits** (usually 8, 16, 32, or 64).

The main goals:
- Support negative numbers
- Allow binary addition/subtraction to work uniformly
- Avoid having two representations of 0

---

## 🧠 Key Concepts

### 1. **Positive Numbers**
They're represented just like regular binary:
```text
Decimal:  5
Binary:   00000101 (8-bit)
```

### 2. **One's Complement (NOT Final)**
To represent `-x`, one method is to flip all the bits of `x`:
```text
Decimal:  -5 (One's complement)
Binary:   11111010 (flip of 00000101)
```
⚠️ Problem: One's complement has **two zeros** (`00000000` and `11111111`) which complicates arithmetic.

### 3. **Two's Complement (Final & Used)**
Two's complement solves this by **adding 1** to the one's complement:
```text
Step 1:  One's complement of 5 → 11111010
Step 2:  Add 1 → 11111011
Result:  -5 in two's complement = 11111011
```

Now:
```text
+5 = 00000101
-5 = 11111011
```

---

## ✅ Why It Works

The real magic of two's complement is that it makes addition work automatically:
```python
# Python example:
5 + (-5) = 00000101 + 11111011 = 00000000  (carry is discarded)
```

It also removes the need for separate subtraction logic.

---

## 🔄 Range of Values
In N-bit two's complement:
- Minimum = `-2^(N-1)`
- Maximum = `2^(N-1) - 1`

Example (8-bit):
```text
Range = -128 to +127
```

---

## 🧩 Fast Algorithm for Two’s Complement of x
1. Start with binary of `x`
2. Invert all bits
3. Add 1

Or:
> Find the first `1` from the right and **leave it and everything after unchanged**, then invert the rest.

---

## 📌 Takeaways
- Two's complement ensures **only one zero**
- Allows unified addition/subtraction logic
- Used in virtually every CPU architecture
- Formula: `~x + 1 = -x`
